# Examples

Quality is easier to copy than to describe. Machine-readable shape: [examples/shape.md](examples/shape.md). After the first live roast, you can replace that file with a tightened real one.

## Mush vs kill mechanism

**Mush (reject):**
> The market is competitive and execution is everything. AI will commoditize this, but you never know.

**Kill mechanism (prefer):**
> Dentists already get chart notes bundled in their PMS. You are a $12/mo feature they will not switch for. At $15/mo you need ~110–170 paying users for the side-income bar; this category converts at “nice to have.”

## Fake alternative vs actual variation

**Fake (reject):**
> Niche down to “AI meeting notes for *pediatric* dentists.”

**Actual (prefer):**
> Stop selling software. Sell a weekly English-language notes cleanup as a done-for-you service to three English-speaking clinic managers you can already message, billed as a retainer. Different buyer motion, different revenue shape, still 1 person.

## Cheap tests

**Vanity (reject):**
> Build an MVP and see if people like it. Put up a waitlist.

**Falsifying (prefer):**
> This week: message 15 clinic managers (not friends). Offer to clean last week’s notes for $150, delivered by Friday. Kill if fewer than 2 pay. Likes and “send me a link” do not count.

## Unlikely win

**Fake (reject):** watering a kill into “but if you execute well, this could work” and slapping a test on the main idea.

**Real (almost never):** the market roast fails to find a kill mechanism *and* the constraints overlay does not kill it either. Then write the words **unlikely win**, say the idea is not so bad / could actually be a really good idea, and only then give the main idea a cheap test.

## Voice

Roast the idea. Do not roast the person.

**No:**
> Only you would think dentists want another dashboard.

**Yes:**
> This is a dashboard for people who already have a dashboard, and they will not open the second one. Cute hobby. Terrible business.
