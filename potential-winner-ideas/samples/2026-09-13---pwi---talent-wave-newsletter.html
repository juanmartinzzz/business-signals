Canvas 6/6

buyer

Canadian/EU university HR and recruitment offices paying for featured posts in front of US researchers eyeing the exit. Employers are the only proven payer in this space: Nature Careers charges $799–$1,145 per job ad, the Chronicle of Higher Education charges $219/60 days online, and universities run standing recruitment-ad budgets with growing paid-advertising teams.

pain

Obscure-but-funded programs (Impact+ chairs, Choose Europe calls, Safe Place-style intakes) need qualified US applicants and are currently discoverable only via scattered university postings. A warm, niche audience of exit-minded researchers is exactly who they want to reach.

channel

Free biweekly newsletter (Substack/beehiiv) tracking talent-program money and chair openings, seeded via posts in researcher communities and amplified by the hot news peg. Academic newsletter sponsorship is an established format (e.g. AAUP Academe sells newsletter sidebar ads). Audience-from-zero is the open question the test is designed to answer.

money

Featured posts at $300 (test price; Nature-anchored upside toward $800). Side bar is ~$15–25k USD net (~$20–35K gross): at $300 that is ~65–115 posts/yr (~1–2 per week) — a real employer-sales treadmill for side hours; at $800 it drops to ~25–45/yr (~2–4 per month). No subscription upkeep, but revenue is lumpy and news-cycle dependent. Mode: side. Primary bar (~$150k net, ~$200K+ gross) would need ~250+ posts/yr at $800 — a sales team, not a side project.

edge

Speed and focus: a solo operator can ship the first 4 issues in 2 weeks while university comms teams plan quarterly. No incumbent owns the "US exit → Canada/EU programs" niche beat; Nature covers it as news, not as a classifieds channel.

test

Publish 4 manual issues over 2 weeks to a free list, then pitch paid featured posts ($300) to 10 Canadian/EU university HR departments. Kill if zero pay. Subscriber count without a paying employer does not count.
